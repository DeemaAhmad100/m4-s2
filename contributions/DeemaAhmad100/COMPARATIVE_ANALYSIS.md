## 1. My KPI and Chart
My colleague reviewed the performance indicators (KPIs) I built. I had prepared four different dashboards, and she selected the Amman Digital Market – Monthly Revenue Trend as the best one.
She really liked the overall work: the clean design, the clarity of the insights, and especially how I created an interactive interface using the Plotly library with HTML. She found the visualizations intuitive and professional.
However, she suggested one improvement: extend the X-axis to include all 12 months of the year, instead of stopping at June (as we currently only have data up to month 6). This will give a complete yearly view and make the trend easier to evaluate.

## 2. My Partner’s KPI and Chart
I reviewed my partner’s KPI: Total Revenue by Product Category (2024 data).
She created a horizontal bar chart that clearly shows Electronics as the dominant category with the highest revenue ($303,289), followed by much smaller contributions from Accessories, Audio, and Peripherals.
I gave her feedback on the design: the original colors were random and did not add any meaning. I recommended using a color gradient instead — starting with lighter colors for lower-revenue categories and progressing to darker, more intense colors for higher-revenue ones (with the strongest color on Electronics). This change makes the ranking instantly visible and turns the chart into a more intuitive performance communication tool.

## 3. Where We Agreed
Both of our analyses clearly show that the Amman Digital Market suffers from high concentration:

My dashboard (Revenue by City) shows that Amman dominates the revenue, with other cities contributing much less.
My partner’s chart (Revenue by Product Category) shows that Electronics accounts for the vast majority of revenue, while other categories remain very small.

We both agree that this lack of diversification — both geographically and by product — represents a significant risk to the business. If anything affects Amman’s economy or demand for Electronics drops, the overall performance could be heavily impacted. We fully agree that the company needs to work on diversifying both its geographic reach and its product portfolio for greater stability.

## 4. Where We Disagreed

While we agreed on the problem of concentration, we differed in our main focus and recommendations:

I focused more on the geographic risk, stressing that depending too heavily on one city (Amman) makes the business vulnerable to local market changes or disruptions.
My partner focused more on the product side, recommending that the company should invest in growing the weaker categories (especially Peripherals and Audio) to reduce over-reliance on Electronics.

In terms of visualization style:

I preferred using a strong single highlight (e.g. gold or distinct color for Amman) to quickly draw the executive’s attention to the most critical insight.
My partner preferred a smooth gradient across all bars to show relative performance in a more balanced way.

I believe my highlighting approach is more effective for executive-level presentations because it delivers the key message faster, while she felt the gradient gives a fairer visual comparison.

## 5. What I Learned
This peer review exercise taught me the importance of looking at business performance from multiple dimensions at the same time. Relying on only geographic analysis or only product analysis can hide important risks.
In future dashboard projects, I will aim to integrate both geographic and product views into one unified dashboard from the start. Combining insights this way gives a much more complete and balanced picture.
